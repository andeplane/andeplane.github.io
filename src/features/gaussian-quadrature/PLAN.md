# Teaching plan

Audience: curious technical readers who know what an integral means but have not studied quadrature.

1. Establish integral, estimate, nodes and weights with a signed-area plot. Separate a quadrature rule from the function it samples.
2. Give the reader two symmetric samples. Predict, move, compare. Match constants, odd powers and the second moment; derive 1/sqrt(3).
3. Explain linearity: matching the four monomials integrates every cubic. Show failure at degree four.
4. Generalize using orthogonal polynomials, with the proof behind an optional disclosure. Explain why 2n-1 is optimal.
5. Compare three methods using exactly the same number of evaluations; include smooth and nonsmooth examples and a movable narrow feature with an analytic reference integral.
6. Map finite intervals and distinguish weight density from discrete weights. A selectable family reference covers Legendre, Jacobi, Laguerre and Hermite with assumptions.
7. Connect to neural operators without claiming recovery of unmeasured information or guaranteed speedups.

Checks: moment exactness through degree 2n-1, positive weights summing to 2, failure at degree 2n, mapped interval; desktop/mobile layout, keyboard controls, valid KaTeX. Sources stay adjacent to claims. No unsolicited publishing or changes to research lessons.
