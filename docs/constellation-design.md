# The light we leave — reviewable storyboard

Draft for feedback, 25 October 2026. This document is the proposed next story,
not a claim that this sequence is already implemented or published.

## Confirmed facts and requirements

- Mikael is your brother. His life ends at 40, when you are 38.
- Include a sister and brother, childhood friend, college friend, first love,
  and an enduring partner who changes from girlfriend/boyfriend to wife/husband.
- Choose the partner woman/man 50/50 once per viewing, not every frame.
- No lyrics, singing, spoken narration, humming, or choir. Extend the existing
  instrumental; do not replace its musical identity.
- Preserve the visible scrolling age y-axis. No banner, sidebar, playback bar,
  or button bar. Brief in-scene text is essential story content.
- Ending: lower/rotate the view toward the lifeline plane, leave remembered
  people behind, and watch descendants continue and branch into the future.
  The camera eventually rests there. Never restart the life or loop the music.
- Commit individual implementation changes and preserve earlier good versions.

## Draft facts — not assumptions about the actual family

Every date except Mikael’s death is illustrative and open for feedback. In
particular: sister’s relative age; dates of partners, marriage and children;
all other deaths; and future descendants. No cause of death is invented.
The draft keeps the earlier sample’s parental and grandparent dates so they
can be reviewed explicitly instead of silently treated as biographical facts.

The age column means your age while alive, then years since your birth. The
ending can stop labelling future time as your personal age once your trail ends.

## Relationship direction

Horizontal distance conveys shared presence and attention, not how much people
are loved. Every person has a continuous path. Positions are evaluated each
year and interpolated smoothly; labels follow their own stars.

- Mom and Dad remain a close pair while both live. In childhood you sit inside
  their cluster. You separate in adolescence and the twenties, then approach
  again around 38–45. Their relationship is not broken to make space for yours.
- Mikael and your sister belong to the childhood cluster. They gain their own
  space as adults. Mikael becomes a permanent remembered star at your age 38.
- Friends enter beside you and drift away when that shared chapter ends.
  First love parts ways; that is distinct from death and creates no memorial.
- The enduring partner is closest during dating, stays a recognisable pair at
  marriage, spreads moderately during busy early parenting, then approaches
  again as children become independent. Busyness must not look like a breakup.
- Children emerge close to both parents. Their paths repeat the broad movement
  toward independence rather than remaining attached forever.
- Death fixes a light at the end of its own lived path. The ending camera moves;
  the deceased do not relocate into an arbitrary decorative polygon.

## Other relationships worth considering

These are proposals, not additions to the confirmed personal cast. Prefer depth
for the existing people over a catalogue of extra names.

1. **The same friend returning.** A college friend can drift away during the
   busy thirties, then reconnect around 44–46 and accompany later life. This
   shows that distance can be temporary. Reuse the same identity rather than
   inventing a new label. A departure is distinct from death.
2. **Mikael within the living family.** Give his path shared childhood years,
   adult independence and renewed closeness. If the draft children remain,
   show the sibling cluster becoming their aunt/uncle network before 38.
   Do not invent personality traits, anecdotes or a cause of death.
3. **The partner’s existing world.** Their path has history before meeting you.
   A few subdued background paths can suggest their family and friends, so
   the relationship feels like two lives meeting. Avoid two more prominent
   in-law labels unless their role matters to this specific story.
4. **A mentor/teacher, optionally.** A short encounter can gently change your
   path’s direction. It should earn a distinct moment, not behave like another
   interchangeable school-friend label.
5. **A pet, only if personally meaningful.** A steady companion could travel
   beside the household. Do not add a pet or another death merely for emotion.

Recommended first refinement: deepen the sibling network and let one existing
friend return. Keep mentor, in-laws and pet as optional rather than increasing
the cast all at once. Mikael and the enduring family remain central.

## Pacing proposal

Aim for roughly two minutes with the instrumental already extended in Suno.
Use more time for the early relationships, about seven seconds at Mikael’s
loss, a brief hold at your own ending, and roughly the last thirty seconds for
life beyond you. Time need not advance at a uniform number of seconds per year.
The future initially accelerates, then settles into a calm indefinite view.

## Ending composition

After your trail ends, lower the camera smoothly toward the plane, looking in
the direction of time. Keep near history and remembered stars in the foreground
and behind the advancing family. Future generations branch from their parents’
paths, increase in number and recede toward a vanishing point. These become
mostly unnamed paths, so the frame does not fill with unreadable labels.

Finish the camera movement rather than spinning forever. The final reminder
appears below the scene, then yields to “For my brother, Mikael.” The music
finishes once. The future continues gently, with no replay or fade to black.
