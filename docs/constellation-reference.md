## Four different kinds of motion

1. **Passing encounters.** Kindergarten friend moves diagonally from left to
   right through the current-age row. It never stops or turns upward into a
   companion lane. Its trail reaches outside the frame. Grade school friend, Middle school friend, High school friend and
   College friend provide other brief crossings. Coworker weaves back and forth
   through the group before leaving, rather than making one straight pass.
2. **Companionship.** Childhood friend enters from the right, turns upward and
   travels beside Me for years. First love arrives from the left and shares a
   nearby course. Wife enters from the left later and remains close. Son begins
   beside the couple rather than entering from an unrelated offscreen life.
3. **Parting while alive.** Childhood friend and First love turn outward and
   leave the frame. Their time keeps moving; they do not become fixed stars.
   Son eventually moves outward but continues visibly into the future.
4. **Death and remembrance.** The moving path ends, while the person's light
   rises into the upper sky as a small, quieter star. Remembered people remain
   present while the living continue. Their stars connect into a constellation
   near the close.

The important distinction is the *shape of the trajectory*. A passing person
keeps sideways momentum. A companion turns to share a course. A person leaving
the relationship turns away but keeps living. A death ends the life path.

## Visual composition

- Living heads share the current-age row; trails below record the past.
- A sparse star field and dark blue background leave the paths easy to read.
- Warm gold marks Me; coral Mom, blue Dad, pink Wife and cyan Son are distinct.
  Remembered stars have smaller, quieter glows and retain only a hint of color.
- Thin bright cores with soft surrounding light give the trails definition.
- Names follow their own dots consistently. They are usually above, with some
  passing names below and short vertical offsets when companions become close.
- Story prose is sparse and appears at the end; the video does most of the work.

## Application to our experience

The added Kindergarten friend uses a continuous left-to-right crossing near You
at age three. It has no close-companion plateau and no death marker. Its stable,
single-line name sits below the present row and fades as it leaves. The lasting
Childhood friend remains separate. No additional prose card is needed.

We retain the user's deliberately different story: You, siblings, the brother's
loss at your age 38, the two-minute instrumental, six short captions, and the

future are extensions requested by the user, not behaviors inferred from this
