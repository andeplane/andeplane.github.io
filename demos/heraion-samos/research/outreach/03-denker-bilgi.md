# 3 — Prof. Ahmet Denker (İstanbul Bilgi University)

**To:** ahmet.denker@bilgi.edu.tr
**Subject:** Your reconstruction of the Heraion (Polykrates) Temple — enquiry about the model files

---

Dear Prof. Denker,

I have been reading your paper "Digital Cultural Heritage: Applications of 3D
Computer Graphics in Reconstructing the Lost Reality of the Temples of Ionia"
(*Journal of Naval Science and Engineering*, 2015, 11:3, 26–42), and I am writing
about Figure 4, "Reconstruction of Heraion (Polykrates) Temple".

I am developing an augmented-reality experience for visitors to the Heraion of
Samos, built around the Great Temple. In surveying what already exists, your
reconstruction is the only complete digital treatment of that specific building I
have been able to find anywhere in the public record — the excavation projects'
digital work is scoped to the prehistoric settlement, and there is no public 3D
asset of the temple at all. That makes your model unusually important to this
project.

My questions are:

1. Do the source files still exist — the scene, geometry, materials and textures,
   in whatever application you built them in?
2. If they do, would you consider licensing or sharing them for a visitor-facing
   AR application at the site? I would need permission to derive an optimised
   runtime asset (GLB/USDZ) from the geometry, and I would credit you in the app
   in whatever form you specify.
3. Could you tell me what the reconstruction was based on — which plans,
   publications and dimensional sources you worked from, and which elements
   (roof profile, sculptural programme, the unfinished portions) you treated as
   conjecture? Even if no files can be shared, this would let me follow your
   reasoning rather than guess at it, and let me mark uncertainty honestly for
   visitors instead of presenting hypothesis as fact.
4. Your paper reconstructs four great Ionic temples. If the models form a set, I
   would be interested to know whether they survive together — though the Heraion
   is the one I need.

If the files are long gone, question 3 is the one I would most value an answer to,
and it costs you only a paragraph.

I should say that the intended outcome is a visitor-facing application and I do
not yet know whether it will be freely distributed or run on a commercial or
sponsored basis. I would rather say so now than come back to renegotiate terms.

With thanks, and with genuine appreciation for the work — reconstructions of this
kind are exactly what makes a site like the Heraion legible to a visitor standing
in front of a single surviving column.
