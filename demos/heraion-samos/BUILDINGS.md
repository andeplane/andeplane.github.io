# Buildings of the Heraion of Samos

All 34 numbered monuments of the [Heraion of Samos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heraion_of_Samos),
following the official numbering in the ODAP visitor booklet, with what the model
does for each and how much of it is evidence.

The sanctuary is a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/595/)
together with Pythagoreion, on the island of [Samos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samos)
off the coast of Asia Minor. It was sacred to [Hera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hera),
who was said to have been born there.

## Legend

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Reconstructed** | Standing three-dimensional reconstruction: platform, columns, entablature |
| **Built up** | Walls raised to an attested height, but no detailed elevation |
| **Footprint** | Plan outline at ground level only |

| Evidence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Attested** | Dimensions stated in a text source we hold |
| **Derived** | Computed from an attested figure |
| **Eyeballed** | Read off the ODAP site plan by eye; ±5 m on position, worse on size |

Positions are metres from the centre of the Great Temple, `east / south`.

| # | Building | Status | Size (m) | Evidence | Position |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---:|
| 6 | **Great Altar** — the cult centre, and the reason the temples face east. A courtyard walled on three sides and open west toward the temple, with a relief frieze of beasts and sphinxes inside and a fireproof green serpentine cap. The ash heap on top was compared by Pausanias to the altar of Zeus at Olympia. | **Built up** | 16.5 × 36.5, wall 5–7 high | Attested | 123 / −1 |

The altar is the only sanctuary building raised above ground level in the model,
because its wall height is the only one attested.

## What the model reconstructs in full

Only building **1**. It is the only one with enough evidence in the archive to
justify standing geometry, and raising thirty-three more would bury the one
reconstruction that is actually argued for.

What is attested about it:

- [dipteral](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipteros) — a double ring of columns —
  55.16 × 108.63 m
- **155 columns** in four sizes and types; 24 along each flank in a double row,
  triple colonnades at the facades, 8 east and 9 west
- columns 20 m high, of marble, on a structure of poros stone
- shafts **unfluted** — unusual, and the most distinctive thing about the
  building; the earlier Rhoikos temple *was* fluted
- bases with horizontal fluting on spira and torus, the Samian type
- a carved, painted band at the head of each shaft
- [Ionic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionic_order) volute capitals outside,
  ovolo-moulded capitals inside
- the architrave was **wooden**, which is why the intercolumniations can be so
  wide and why there was never a continuous stone frieze at this scale
- one column survives [in situ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_situ) on the
  south flank

What is invented: the [krepidoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crepidoma), the
entablature depths, the [cella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cella) plan, and
the roof — which is off by default, because the building was never finished.
