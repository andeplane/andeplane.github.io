Analogue computing with water

The Water GPT

Language models are mostly matrix multiplications, and a grid of pipes and valves can do one with nothing but water. This page simulates that water, pipe by pipe, and runs real language models on it: calc-gpt, TinyStories and GPT-2.

Each reservoir on the left holds water at a head (height) xi. It feeds a horizontal manifold. Where the manifold crosses a collector column, a thin pipe with a valve joins them. In a thin pipe the flow is laminar, so Hagen–Poiseuille gives flow = conductance × head difference: q = Gji xi.

Each column drains into a collector held near zero head. Water is conserved, so a column's outflow is the sum of everything its valves let in: yj = Σi Gji xi. That is a whole matrix–vector product, and it happens all at once.

Valves can only let water through, so each output uses a +/− pair of columns: a positive weight opens the valve on the + column, a negative one on the − column, and the answer is the difference.

    What is water and what is digital

    • water Every matrix product: the embedding lookup (one reservoir per character opens its row of valves), Q·K·V, attention scores QKᵀ and attention·V (valves set on the fly from the activations), the attention output, MLP up and down, and the output head.
    • digital The glue between crossbars: flowmeters read the collectors and regulators set the next heads. LayerNorm, softmax, the 1/√d scale, bias and residual adds, ReLU and the final argmax are done in digital arithmetic.
    The water you see follows the network solution: every valve flow, manifold flow and collector level comes from solving Kirchhoff's law for laminar flow in the whole grid of pipes. The numbers are the same ones the model uses.
