Analogue computing with water

The Water GPT

An analogue computer doesn't count in digits. It lets a law of physics do the arithmetic. Here the physics is water: a grid of pipes and valves multiplies a matrix by a vector in one go, and scaled up, it runs a real language model.

Everything you see is a physics simulation of that water. Every number the models use comes out of a simulated pipe, valve or tank.

Each reservoir on the left is held at a fixed level xi (engineers call it the hydraulic head): it is topped up and has an overflow, so its pressure never changes. That pressure is the input. Its supply pipe runs to the right and crosses a row of collector columns. At every crossing a valve lets water through. Slow water in a thin pipe flows in proportion to the pressure behind it (Hagen–Poiseuille), so a valve of conductance w passes a steady w · xi litres per second. The valve's conductance is the weight.

Each column drains into a collector that is open to the air, so nothing pushes back. The water never stops, but within a moment every flow is steady, and since water is conserved, the steady flow rate into a collector is the sum of what its valves pass: yj = Σi wji xi. That rate is the answer: under each collector, a flowmeter's float rises to show it. A whole matrix–vector product, with no part waiting for another.

A valve can only let water through, so each output uses a +/− pair of collectors: a positive weight opens the valve on the + collector, a negative one on the − collector, and the answer is the difference. (Inputs can be negative too, so the bigger machines give each input two reservoirs, x⁺ and x⁻.)

Not just a water toy. Analogue AI chips being built today use exactly this layout with electricity: voltages for levels, resistors for valves, and currents adding up in a wire, by Ohm's and Kirchhoff's laws. Water obeys the same laws, and you can watch it.

What is water and what is not

  • crossbars Every matrix product: the embedding lookup (one reservoir per character opens its row of valves), Q·K·V, attention scores QKᵀ and attention·V (valves set on the fly from the activations), the attention output, MLP up and down, and the final scores.
  • circuits Everything in between, also water. Softmax: a check-valve node finds the largest score, valves profiled to open as es/√d pass the rest, and a fixed inflow splits across them in exactly the softmax ratios. LayerNorm: an averaging node gives the mean, orifices give the square and the square root, and valves opened in proportion to σ divide by it. ReLU: a check valve. Bias and residual adds are flows merging. Between crossbars, pressure regulators (mechanical, no electronics) set each next reservoir's level from a collector's flow. Every part has its own manufacturing error.
  • read-out Water too: every output collector feeds one node through its own check valve, and the collector that fills it is the next character.
  • not water Only the keyboard: each character you type opens its reservoir.
The water you see follows the network solution: every valve flow, supply-pipe flow and collector level comes from solving Kirchhoff's law for laminar flow in the whole grid of pipes. Every number the model uses comes out of a simulated water part, and each part is built with its own manufacturing error. The simulation is a pipe-network model (Hagen–Poiseuille pipes, orifices and check valves at steady state), not a computational fluid dynamics solve.
