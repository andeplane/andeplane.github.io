# Audio provenance

Downloaded 2026-09-11. The recordings are bundled locally. Music/speech excerpts contain the first 90 seconds, converted with FFmpeg to mono 24 kHz / 64 kbps MP3; recordings are not described as original broadcasts unless they actually were broadcasts.

## entertainer.mp3 — 600 kHz / Ragtime

Scott Joplin, *The Entertainer* (1902 composition). Performed by Wikimedia user IE on a Casio WK-3300 in September 2007. This is a modern performance of an old classic, not a historical Joplin performance.

Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Entertainer_-_Scott_Joplin.ogg
Original: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/1/1b/The_Entertainer_-_Scott_Joplin.ogg
Rights: composition public domain; performance explicitly dedicated to the public domain by IE worldwide, with unconditional permission where dedication is not possible.

## rhapsody.mp3 — 750 kHz / Classics

Franz Liszt, *Hungarian Rhapsody No. 2*, part I. Sergei Rachmaninoff, 1919 Edison recording. Edison Diamond Disc 82169-R, originally sourced by Commons from the Library of Congress.

Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Second_Hungarian_Rhapsody.ogg
Original: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/37/Second_Hungarian_Rhapsody.ogg
Rights: Commons identifies the recording as public domain; composition by Liszt (1811–1886), performance from 1919 by Rachmaninoff (1873–1943).

## fireside.mp3 — 900 kHz / Fireside

Franklin D. Roosevelt, first fireside chat, 12 March 1933, on the banking crisis. This is archival speech audio, not an actor or synthesized voice.

Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fireside_Chat_1,_On_the_Banking_Crisis_(March_12,_1933),_Franklin_Delano_Roosevelt_second_edit.ogg
Original: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/3f/Fireside_Chat_1%2C_On_the_Banking_Crisis_%28March_12%2C_1933%29%2C_Franklin_Delano_Roosevelt_second_edit.ogg
Rights: Commons marks this as a US federal government public-domain work. Commons source credits the Miller Center of Public Affairs.
Archive catalogue: https://www.fdrlibrary.org/utterancesfdr
